## Why this exists

Making things is at the heart of a technical university. A prototype turns an assumption into a fact, and nothing teaches like building the thing yourself.

TU Delft has a remarkable collection of places to do that: faculty workshops, research labs, makerspaces, a science centre, student-run studios. But every faculty looks after its own, and there has never been one overview. Students often do not know what exists outside their own building. Staff in different workshops solve the same problems side by side without knowing it. And expensive machines stand idle while someone two buildings away is looking for exactly that machine.

**Maker Resources** is an attempt to fix that with the simplest tool that could work: one open website that lists

- **makerspaces**: where they are, when they are open and who may use them,
- **equipment**: what stands where, and what you need before you may use it,
- **skills**: starting from what you want to do, the places, machines and people that go with it,
- **people**: staff and fellow makers who are happy to be asked,
- **projects**: what others made, and how.

The idea is borrowed with admiration from [MIT's Project Manus](https://make.mit.edu/), which did the same for the makerspaces at MIT.

## A pilot, starting at IDE

The guide started at the Faculty of Industrial Design Engineering (IDE), with the workshops and labs its students use most, and a number of places elsewhere on campus and in the region. The aim is to grow it faculty by faculty until it covers the whole university.

This is a pilot and **not an official TU Delft service**. The information is collected with care, but opening hours, rules and machines change. When in doubt, the workshop staff are always right.

## Kept up to date by its users

A guide like this only works if it stays correct, and a small team cannot visit every workshop every month. So the guide is built to be maintained by the people who know best:

- **Anyone can add or correct something**, without an account, on the [Contribute page](../contribute/).
- **Every page shows when it was last checked.** Pages that have not been looked at for a year say so, and ask the reader for help.
- **Workshop staff can own their page** and edit it directly.
- **Nothing is published without a second pair of eyes**: a maintainer reviews every change.

## Open by design

Everything here is open: the text is published under [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), the software under the MIT licence, and the full history of every change is public. Other universities are welcome to copy the whole thing and fill it with their own workshops.

Photos are the exception: they belong to the people and organisations credited next to them.

## What is next

- More faculties and more machines, starting with the places students ask about most.
- Short "getting started" guides for every skill.
- Links to the booking and training systems the workshops already use.
- A place for student communities built around a skill.

Have an idea, or want your workshop on the list? [Add it](../contribute/), or get in touch through the project's page on GitHub.
